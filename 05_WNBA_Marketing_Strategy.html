Independent marketing strategy analysis

World-class product. Misaligned marketing.

The WNBA has Olympic-calibre athletes, record attention and a landmark media deal, yet its commercial results still trail its talent. I argued the problem is not the product, but a marketing mix whose four parts pull against each other.

The question

Why doesn't attention turn into fans?

The WNBA's revenue gap with the NBA is often read as a talent gap. The evidence points elsewhere: a gap in media investment and in how the league has marketed itself. Its audience is young, digital-first and values-driven, and the marketing mix was not built for them.

Using PESTLE, Five Forces and segmentation to set the context, I diagnosed each of the four Ps and found the same pattern in every one: the parts were working against each other rather than as a single play.

The diagnosis

Four Ps, run as one play

Tap a player to see what was wrong with each part of the mix, and what I proposed instead.

Product

The problem

The core game is world-class, but the augmented layer is thin: little in the way of loyalty programmes, streaming extras, gaming tie-ins or athlete-led content, next to the NBA's 2K and digital ecosystem.

The fix

Invest urgently in the augmented product: athlete-led subscription content, gaming integrations and digital experiences, steered by social listening towards what fans actually engage with.

Diagnosis built on the marketing mix, the three levels of product, psychological and penetration pricing, channel design and integrated marketing communications (Keller, 2016).

The biggest shift

From attention to participation

The largest change I proposed was in promotion. The league was still spending in the attention economy, on paid placements and interruption, while its fans had moved to the participation economy, where content is shared and co-created. Athlete-generated content around Caitlin Clark's 2024 debut showed how much further organic reach could travel than paid media.

Where fans already were

Content strategy to be guided by Berger and Milkman's STEPPS factors, with social currency and emotion as the priorities, and all promotional tools run as one integrated system rather than in silos.

Place

Give every channel one job

Games were spread across broadcast TV, several streaming services and the league's own app, with no hierarchy. Broadcast partners had little reason to promote streaming, and the app was under-resourced. I proposed a three-channel structure in which no channel competes with another.

Communication channel

Broadcast TV

Builds awareness and brings new fans in.

Distribution channel

Streaming

Serves engaged subscribers who want every game.

Direct sales channel

League app

Merchandise, premium content and community, owned by the league.

Outcome

Where it landed

The result is an integrated repositioning plan that changes all four Ps together, built on one diagnosis: the league's commercial gap comes from a misaligned marketing mix, not from the quality of the product.

Open questions

What the analysis could not settle

  1. How much revenue would penetration pricing give up? The plan expects premium tiers and a larger sponsorship base to recapture it, but the timing is untested.
  2. Will broadcast partners accept an awareness-only role? The three-channel structure depends on TV partners supporting a model where streaming and the league app take direct sales.
  3. How should participation be measured? Share rates and athlete-generated reach would need to replace paid impressions as the core marketing metric.
