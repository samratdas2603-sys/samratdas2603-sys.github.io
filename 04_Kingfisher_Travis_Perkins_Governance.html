Independent governance analysis

Same code, very different years

I assessed two FTSE-listed home improvement groups, Kingfisher and Travis Perkins, against the UK Corporate Governance Code 2024, working directly from their annual reports. Both complied on paper. Only one did it without visible strain.

The approach

Reading the reports, not the summaries

Governance statements are written to reassure. The useful evidence sits in the detail: committee sizes, fee changes, when an evaluation actually happened, and who is doing which job. I compared both boards across ten metrics, then tested each against specific provisions of the Code.

The boards

Two tables, set very differently

Kingfisher's ten-member board had a full executive team and an even gender split. Travis Perkins ran most of its year with seven directors and no chief executive, after its CEO left in March 2025.

The verdict

Stronger on paper, and in practice

Kingfisher's board was the stronger of the two. But the judgement needed care: much of the strain at Travis Perkins came from genuine disruption, and its processes largely held.

Kingfisher

Balanced, fully staffed and meeting every diversity target. Its one real issue, a director at the nine-year mark, was already being resolved through a planned departure.

Travis Perkins

It deserves credit for how it responded: the Senior Independent Director stepped in, a new CEO was appointed and committees absorbed extra work. But the year left independence held together by a few people doing several jobs at once.

Recommendations

What each board should do next

Travis Perkins

Commit to measurable interim milestones towards the 40% women target, starting with at least one of the four senior board roles held by a woman by 2026, and disclose progress publicly. Publishing a board skills matrix, as Kingfisher does, would strengthen its case to investors.

Kingfisher

Maintain its gender balance through the planned 2025 departures, and keep publishing its skills matrix so investors can check that diversity holds.

Outcome

Where it landed

The review found Kingfisher's board the stronger of the two, in practice as well as on paper, and set out specific actions for each: interim diversity milestones and a published skills matrix for Travis Perkins, and holding gender balance through planned departures for Kingfisher.

Open questions

What the analysis could not settle

  1. Did Travis Perkins complete its deferred external evaluation? It was pushed to 2026, and what it finds will show whether the board's workarounds held.
  2. Does the Executive Chair arrangement fully unwind? With a new chief executive in place, the Chair's expanded role, and his doubled fee, should return to normal.
  3. Did Kingfisher keep an even gender split? Two long-serving directors were due to leave, which puts its 50% at risk unless succession is planned for it.
